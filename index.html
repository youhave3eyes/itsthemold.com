01

Chapter 01 · The toxins

Meet the molds and their poisons

Mold is a fungus. When it grows on wet drywall, wood or carpet, some kinds make chemicals called mycotoxins to fight off other microbes. Those chemicals don't stay on the wall. They cling to dust and get into the air you breathe.

The usual suspects

6 indoor molds
Stachybotrys“Black mold”

The one that needs a flood

Grows on paper and drywall that stays soaked for a long time. Slimy black or greenish patches.

MakesTrichothecenes such as satratoxins. They stop cells from making proteins.
Linked toLung and nasal irritation; nerve inflammation in animal studies.
AspergillusGreen, yellow, black

The most common indoor mold

Dust, HVAC systems, damp walls, stored food. Hundreds of species. Most are harmless to healthy people; a few make strong toxins.

MakesAflatoxin, ochratoxin, gliotoxin and sterigmatocystin, depending on species.
Linked toLiver damage (aflatoxin), kidney damage (ochratoxin), lung infection in weak immune systems.
PenicilliumBlue-green fuzz

The fridge and carpet mold

Old food, wet carpet, wallpaper, insulation. It spreads fast and gives off that “musty” smell.

MakesOchratoxin, citrinin and others, depending on species.

The sign of a long leak

Shows up next to Stachybotrys on soaked wood, drywall and under floors. Its presence means water sat for a while.

MakesChaetoglobosins and chaetomins.
Linked toCell damage in lab studies; often flagged on indoor mold tests.

Grows on crops like corn and wheat, and indoors on wet carpet, humidifiers and window seals.

Four ways it gets into your head

Mechanisms in humans & animals
01 · Nose to brainAnimal studies

They ride the smell nerve

The science

In mice, satratoxin G (from Stachybotrys) breathed in through the nose killed smell-sensing nerve cells and inflamed the brain's smell center.

In plain words

Your nose is a short, direct road to your brain. Some mold toxins can travel it.

02 · InflammationWell established

They set off alarms

The science

Damp, moldy buildings are clearly linked to airway inflammation and asthma. Inflammation signals (cytokines) can also reach the brain and cause “sickness behavior.”

In plain words

Your immune system fights the mold, and that fight leaves you tired, foggy and achy.

03 · Cell damageLab studies

They jam your cells

The science

Trichothecenes block ribosomes, the machines that build proteins. Ochratoxin and others stress mitochondria, the cell's power plants.

In plain words

Cells that can't build or make energy run slow. Nerve cells feel it first.

04 · Stuck immune systemDebated

The alarm won't turn off

What's being studied

Some doctors describe CIRS (chronic inflammatory response syndrome): a long-running inflammation after living in a water-damaged building. It is not yet widely accepted in mainstream medicine.

In plain words

Some people stay sick even after they leave. Researchers are still working out why.

The biggest clueTry it yourself

Do you feel better away from home?

What to watch

If your stuffy nose, headaches or brain fog ease up on vacation or at work, then come back within a day or two of being home, the building is a suspect.

What to do next

Write down how you feel each day and where you were. Then look for the source: a smell, a stain, an old leak. Chapter 02 shows where to look.

Chapter 02 · Where it hides

Water in. Mold follows.

Mold spores are already in every home. They only grow where there's water. Find the water and you find the mold, often behind a wall, under a floor or inside the AC where you can't see it.

After any leak or flood

24–48 h

The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to stop mold from starting. Use fans and a dehumidifier, and pull up soaked carpet pads.

Keep indoor humidity between 30% and 50%. A cheap humidity meter (hygrometer) tells you where you stand.

Anything porous that stays wet longer, like drywall, carpet pad or insulation, usually has to be cut out and replaced.

03

Chapter 03 · Solutions

Kill it. Then neutralize it.

Most mold cleanup stops at killing the mold. The problem is that dead mold and the toxins it made are still there, in the dust, the air and your vents. A real fix does three things: stops the water, removes the growth and breaks down the toxins left behind.

Dead mold is still toxic.

In a 2005 lab study, chlorine dioxide gas killed Stachybotrys colonies, but the dead mold still tested toxic. A second study found the gas alone didn't break down two trichothecene toxins, while chlorine dioxide dissolved in water did.

Wilson et al., Applied and Environmental Microbiology, 2005 · Wilson et al., International Journal of Toxicology, 2005

So clean the toxins too.

Mycotoxins are sticky and don't wash off with plain water. That's why newer protocols add a cleaner that breaks them down, like hypochlorous acid (HOCl), and a careful wipe-down of every surface where dust settles.

In a test paid for by Superstratum, its HOCl cleaner cut mycotoxins on contaminated wood board by about 94–95%.

Hypochlorous acidHOCl · fog, spray, wipe

Lab tests · company-funded data
What it is
A mild acid your own white blood cells make to kill germs. Made by running salt water through electricity. It breaks down into salt and water.
What it does
Kills mold, bacteria and viruses on contact, and in tests breaks down mycotoxins on surfaces. Gentle enough to use around people when made to the right strength.
How it's used
  • Fog the whole space so the mist reaches the air and every surface.
  • Spray and wipe hard surfaces with microfiber.
  • Fog again, lighter, after the deep clean.
Know this
It loses strength over time and in sunlight, so buy fresh and store it dark. Follow the label for strength. It doesn't fix the leak and can't reach inside walls.

Chlorine dioxide gasClO₂ · “gas bombs” & pouches

Strong at killing mold · weak on toxins as a gas
What it is
A gas made when two chemicals mix in a pouch or packet. It's used to sanitize hospitals, food plants and grow rooms, and sold for odor and mold in homes and cars.
What it does
A gas goes where a spray can't: into cracks, behind baseboards, under cabinets and through soft furniture. It's a strong mold and germ killer.
Where it fits
As a finishing step after the leak is fixed and the rot is removed, to reach spots you can't clean by hand. Follow with HEPA and wipe-down, since the gas alone may leave toxins behind.
Know this
It's a lung irritant. The room must be empty of people, pets and plants. It can bleach fabric and corrode metal and electronics. Use only products labeled for this, exactly as directed.

How a ClO₂ gas treatment goes

Always follow your product's label
  1. PrepFix leaks first. Take out people, pets, plants and food. Unplug and cover electronics.
  2. SealClose windows and inside doors as the label says. Put a warning sign on the door.
  3. Activate & leaveStart it right where it sits, then walk out. Don't carry it or sniff it.
  4. WaitUsually several hours to overnight. Don't open the door early.
  5. Air outWear a mask going in. Open every window, run fans, then wait before staying in.
  6. Clean upHEPA vacuum and wipe surfaces to remove dead mold and leftover toxins.
!

Never drink or swallow chlorine dioxide

Some websites sell chlorine dioxide as a drink or “miracle solution.” The FDA warns that drinking it is the same as drinking bleach and can cause severe vomiting, diarrhea and dangerous dehydration. It is for empty rooms only.

  • Never mix it with other cleaners, acids or ammonia.
  • Never stay in the room or sleep in the home while it's active.
  • The workplace safety limit (OSHA) is just 0.1 parts per million. If you can smell it, leave and air out longer.

Chapter 04 · Your body

Get out. Then clear it out.

Your body can clear mycotoxins on its own, mostly through the liver, the gut and the kidneys. The first and biggest step is to stop breathing them in. No supplement can keep up with a home that's still moldy.

Before you start a detox

  • Binders can also grab medications and nutrients. Space them at least 2 hours apart, and ask your doctor.
  • Binders can cause constipation. Drink plenty of water.

Daily habits that help

  • Run a HEPA air purifier in the bedroom.
  • Keep humidity between 30% and 50%.
  • Eat foods that support the liver, like broccoli, cabbage, garlic and leafy greens.
  • Throw out moldy food, and store grains, nuts and coffee dry and sealed.
